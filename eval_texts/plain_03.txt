Year by year, children’s literature attempts to break down gender stereotype by introducing the concept of gender-neutral such as cross-dressing, although it still delineates a subtle change. The impact of children’s literature on the development of children’s gender identity should not be underestimated because as early as two years old, children realise their sex and start to develop their gender identity. As a result, many children subconsciously see the world through the lens of gender identity and categorise what they observe into masculinity, femininity or gender-neutral in which help them to organise information. It is evidently essential to develop the lens of gender identity to conceive the world and children’s literature will be beneficial to learn gender norm. Thus, children’s literature should avoid depicting a distorted image of gender role on people that can depreciate one’s characteristic or underestimate one’s capability. This can prevent children’s potential growth, lower children’s self-esteem who cannot find characters relatable to themselves or when characters they can reflect are negatively portrayed, and/or spread contorted value to other readers. The idea of cross-dressing in children’s literature can facilitate to resolve the gender stereotype and improve the development of children’s gender identity as it brings in the value of gender-neutral: the young readers are liberated from conventional gender roles and can express their gender identities freely regarding behaviour, clothing, and language tone. As more gender-neutral children’s books are published, society could change into a more liberal and diverse community that more people could open their true selves in the future.
